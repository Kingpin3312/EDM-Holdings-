# Six client names in the public capability statement — Christopher's call

**Raised by the technical audit, 22 August 2026. Not changed, because this is a
commercial decision rather than a defect.**

`capability-statement-public.html`, page 17, states:

> We work as a specialist interior package partner to Tier-1 main contractors and
> consultancies, including:
> **JLL · Gilbert-Ash · Graham · John Sisk & Son · McLaughlin & Harvey · Farrans**

Whoever wrote this was consent-aware — there is a comment in the source
immediately below removing PMK Group *because consent is verbal only*. The other
six were left in.

**The question:** the consent process exists because naming a client is a claim a
Tier-1 supply-chain team can and does verify. `consents/` is empty. Six firms are
named in the document EDM hands to procurement, and none of them is in the
consent register.

**Three options.**

1. **Confirm the position and file it.** If these are genuine past relationships
   under the E&D Drywall heritage and there is no objection to being listed,
   record that in `consents/` and the document stands as written. This is the
   strongest outcome — the names do real work.
2. **Generalise.** Replace the list with the shape rather than the names:
   *"Tier-1 main contractors and consultancies across commercial, hotel,
   education and data-centre work — references available on request."*
   Loses some weight, carries no risk.
3. **Ask.** The same email that goes to PMK Group can go to these six. The draft
   is at `13-Go-To-Market/LinkedIn/PMK-Consent-Email.md`.

**Recommendation: option 2 now, option 1 when the consents are filed.** A named
list you cannot evidence is the kind of finding that ends an onboarding quietly,
and the document is strong without it. But this is a business call and the
document has board sign-off as written, so it has been left alone pending your
decision.

`tools/compliance-check.py` currently reports these names. Once you decide, either
file the consents or make the edit, and the check goes quiet.
