The mark

One logo, used properly.

Our logo is the three-tone cube alongside the EDM Holdings wordmark and the line that says exactly what we do. Used together they are the primary lockup — the version to reach for first.

The cube is built from three faces: the dark emerald front, and two greys that give it depth. Those greys are part of the mark. Don't swap them for the accent green or flatten them to one tone.

Clear space
Keep a margin around the logo at least as tall as the "E" in EDM on every side. Nothing — text, images or edges — should crowd it.
Minimum size
Don't reproduce the full lockup below 30 mm wide in print, or 150 px on screen. Below that, use the cube on its own.
Source files
Always use the masters in 12-Logo-Masters or 01-Brand-Identity/Logo. Never recreate the cube and never redraw the wordmark.
Brand Guidelines02
Variants

Three versions, one identity.

Primary — on white
The full lockup on a white ground. Use it wherever there is room. This is the default for documents, letterheads, presentations and the website.
Reversed — on emerald
For an emerald or dark ground. Use sparingly: pages and covers stay white with emerald as the accent. Bands, footers and table header rows are the exception.
Cube mark — icons and small sizes
For favicons, app icons, avatars and anywhere the wordmark would be too small to read. In a social feed the mark renders at around 48–60 px, so the cube alone is correct there.
Choosing between them

If the wordmark would fall below 150 px on screen or 30 mm in print, you want the cube. If in doubt, put both versions side by side at the size they will actually appear and pick the one that reads.

Brand Guidelines03
Getting it wrong

Please don't.

The quickest way to make the brand look cheap is to alter the logo. A few firm rules.

Don't recolour the cube or the wordmark, or change the two panel greys.

Don't rebuild the lockup or move the wordmark around the cube.

Don't stretch, squash or rotate the logo.

Don't place it on a busy photograph or a low-contrast colour.

Don't add shadows, gradients, outlines or glows.

Don't use the old bronze or bone versions. They are retired.

Where this has actually gone wrong

Retired bone tints survived as the background of the workwear and vehicle livery renders as late as V11, and the livery swatch bar printed an off-system green as if it were the brand emerald. Both corrected in V12. The lesson is that mockups get sent to suppliers — check them against this page before anyone quotes from them.

Brand Guidelines05
Typography

Montserrat, throughout.

One typeface does everything — headlines, body, captions. It is clean, modern and easy to read at any size. Keep it consistent and don't introduce a second font.

Aa Bb Cc
MONTSERRAT  ·  LIGHT 300  ·  REGULAR 400  ·  SEMIBOLD 600  ·  BOLD 700

Headlines

Bold (700), tight tracking. Short and direct.

Body

Regular (400), generous line spacing for easy reading.

Labels

Bold, uppercase, wide tracking, in emerald.

Four weights, and only four

Light 300, Regular 400, SemiBold 600, Bold 700. Medium 500 is not in the system. The V11 capability statement and brochure used Medium — if you see a 500 weight anywhere, it is an error, not a variant.

Embedding
Montserrat is embedded in the website, the documents and the templates, so it renders correctly everywhere. Never rely on a font being installed on someone else's machine.
Watch for glyphs
A tick, a cross or an arrow typed as a character will pull a fallback typeface into the file and break the one-font rule silently. Draw them instead. Check any exported PDF with pdffonts: only Montserrat should be listed.
Brand Guidelines06
How we sound

Plain, direct, specific.

  • Say what we do and what we've done. No jargon, no filler.
  • Lead with the point. Short sentences do more work than long ones.
  • Be specific — a real project, a real number, a real trade.
  • Confident, not boastful. We let the track record carry it.
  • UK English, always. Programme, metre, colour, organisation.
  • No absolutes. Nothing is "100%", "guaranteed", "world-class" or "leading".
Words we don't use

sublet  ·  sub-letting  ·  "let's talk about your package"  ·  thrilled  ·  excited to announce  ·  delighted to share  ·  reach out  ·  leverage  ·  seamless  ·  robust  ·  world-class
