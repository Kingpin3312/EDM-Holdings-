A word from the Chief Executive

We build the inside of buildings, and we do it with our own people.

That sentence is the whole business, and almost every decision we have taken follows from it.

Most interior packages get split across four or five trade subcontractors. Everyone in construction knows what happens next. The gaps between those contracts are where programme slips, where quality arguments start, and where a main contractor ends up refereeing a dispute they had no part in causing.

We decided to take those gaps into our own scope instead. One contract, one team on site, one standard from setting out to final snag. It is not a clever idea. It is simply harder to run, which is why it stays rare.

Directly employing our workforce costs more on paper than buying labour when we need it. It also means we know our real outputs, our people turn up because they work for us, and the promises we make are ours to keep rather than someone else's to break.

The other decision worth explaining is the record. Fire-stopping, head-of-wall detail and everything else inside a wall disappears the day it is boarded. We photograph and sign it before it goes, and hand the whole record over as a pack. Clients rarely ask for that at tender stage. They are extremely glad of it eighteen months later when a consultant wants evidence.

Our people have worked in this trade since 1986. Most of what we know was learned the hard way on sites in the UK and Ireland, and it now runs to the same standard here in the UAE.

If you are pricing an interior package, send us the drawings and the programme. You will get a straight answer, the same working day.

Damien Meenan
Chief Executive, EDM Holdings
Corporate Brochure03
The model

Every split in a package is an interface. Every interface is a risk somebody carries.

A typical interior package is let to four or five specialists. Each is competent. Each holds a different programme, a different quality plan and a different view of where their scope ends.

The gaps between them are where projects lose time. A ceiling grid set out before the partition line is confirmed. A glazed screen ordered to a dimension that moved. Fire-stopping that belongs to whoever was there last. None of it shows on a tender comparison, and all of it lands on the main contractor's desk as a coordination problem nobody priced.

One programme
Sequencing between trades is an internal scheduling matter for us rather than a negotiation between subcontractors with different incentives.
One quality plan
The same inspection stages and tolerances from setting out to final decoration. The standard doesn't change hands halfway through the floor.
One point of responsibility
There is no interface argument to referee, because there is nobody to argue with. If something in the interior is wrong, it is ours.
One set of records
Produced by one system as the work happens, rather than assembled from four suppliers at the end.
One commercial conversation
A single valuation, a single variation account and a single final account for the whole interior.
Self-delivery costs more than the cheapest combination of subcontract prices. It costs less by handover, and the difference never appears on the tender comparison.

The trades

What we install.

Drywall partitions & ceilings

Metal-stud partitions, shaft walls, MF and suspended ceilings, dry lining and encasements. Set out from a survey of what was actually built, and built square the first time.

Fire & acoustic-rated systems

Built to the certified detail — the tested arrangement, not the convenient one — and documented as we go, so they stand up to inspection at handover and to diligence afterwards.

Timber wall & ceiling panels

Veneered and slatted finishes, acoustic-backed where required. Specified for how they look, and capable of real acoustic work when the build-up behind them is right.

Aluminium & glazing

Single and double-glazed partition systems, framed or frameless, to open up the floorplate. Performance decided at the head, the floor detail and the void above, not by the glass alone.

Painting & decoration

Commercial-grade paint and decorative finishes to a defined, handover-ready level — agreed against a benchmark panel up front, not argued at the end.

Joinery

Reception counters, tea points, storage and feature joinery, made to drawing and fitted by the same team that built the walls around it — which is why the junctions work.

Every trade above is delivered under EDM supervision to one programme, one quality plan and one point of responsibility.

Corporate Brochure05
The craft

Drywall looks simple from a distance.

Most of the cost risk in an interior package sits in a small number of details, and every one of them is decided before anybody starts boarding.

Setting out
From a survey of the as-built structure, not the architectural drawing alone. Slabs get poured out of tolerance and existing buildings are never what the record says. Half a day proving the grid prevents a floor of walls sitting three millimetres out against joinery made to drawing.
Head-of-wall
Partitions are rigid; slabs deflect. If the head detail doesn't allow for the movement the structural design predicts, the board cracks along the top or the studs take load they were never designed for. Right gap, right fixing centres, fire-stopped to the tested arrangement.
Fire-stopping
The least visible work in the package and the most consequential. The right product, in the right gap, at every point a service crosses a compartment line — photographed and signed before it is covered, because the only way to check it afterwards is to cut into the wall.
Risers and shaft walls
Where drywall and services have their argument. Most risers can only be built from one side, which changes the system, the sequence and the access. On paper there is always room, because on paper nothing has a bracket or a tolerance.
Acoustic continuity
A partition performs at its junctions, not in the middle of the panel. A well-built acoustic wall with an open ceiling void above the line performs about as well as a doorway.
None of this is unusual knowledge. It is what a firm learns by being wrong on a live job, and it is priced into what an experienced contractor quotes.
Corporate Brochure06
Sectors

The trades are the same everywhere. The demands are not.

Workplace
Cat A and Cat B. Grade-A floors where the finish is the product. The expensive part is the seam: Cat A ceilings and services designed with no knowledge of the Cat B layout, so the tenant's partitions land under a diffuser and half the grid comes back out. We deliver both scopes, which means we can say at Cat A stage which decisions get expensive later.
Residential
Repetition at volume. Output rates, consistent detailing and unit-by-unit sign-off matter more than any single feature. A detail that is thirty seconds slower is a fortnight across a tower.
Hotels & leisure
Acoustic separation between keys is the whole job, and it is decided at the junctions. Heavy feature finishes, and a handover date usually tied to a trading opening that will not move.
Education
Fixed holiday windows with no tolerance either side, hard-wearing detailing built for use rather than for a photograph, and compliance documentation that actually gets read.
Environments

New build, refurbishment, occupied buildings and live operational facilities — including permit-controlled areas with restricted access and out-of-hours working. Strip-out is delivered as part of the package, because the team clearing the floor is the team that has to build on it.

Mission-critical delivery

What a data centre teaches a fit-out contractor.

Our current UAE delivery is a thirteen-zone interior package inside a 100MW data centre, as subcontractor to a Tier-1 main contractor.

The trades are the same as any fit-out: partitions, boards, fire-stopping, finishes. What changes is the tolerance, the inspection regime and the consequence of getting it wrong. Everything is documented, everything is witnessed, and nothing is closed in on a promise.

The works are structured across thirteen zones, each built, inspected and handed over in sequence, so the wider programme keeps moving while we deliver. The following trades get a finished area rather than an assurance.

Tolerance
Tighter than commercial fit-out, and checked rather than assumed.
Inspection
Witnessed and recorded at each stage. A zone does not close on anyone's word.
Coordination
Daily, with every trade working around us in a live and permit-controlled environment.
Handover
Zone by zone, with the record complete on the day rather than assembled at the end.
A firm that can hold that discipline on a mission-critical site can hold it comfortably on a Grade-A office floor. It rarely transfers the other way.

Corporate Brochure08
Evidence

Four systems, built in-house, that follow one wall from tender to handover.

Most subcontractors price on one system, build on another and report on a third, and none of them talk to each other. We built our own instead, because the thread between them is where the commercial value sits.

01 · Price it

EDM Measure

Drawing-based takeoff. Every wall run traced and landed in a live, priced bill against a wall reference, built from real wall build-ups rather than a rate per square metre.

Load a new revision over the old and what changed is highlighted — so a variation is measured before anyone argues about whether there is one.

02 · Prove it

EDM Evidence

Eight inspection stages per wall, signed and dated by the supervisor who looked at it. The two stages that matter — services closed in, and fire-stopping — will not accept a pass without a photograph.

Failed checks stay on the record beside the passes. That is what makes a pass worth something.

03 · Report it

EDM Progress

Planned stage dates by zone form the programme; actual progress fills in from what supervisors have already signed. Percentage complete comes from records, not a walk-round.

Hold-ups are logged the day they happen and closed when they clear. That dated log is the extension-of-time conversation.

04 · Record it

EDM Daybook

One page a day on the site manager's phone. Labour, weather, deliveries, snags — and every instruction received, with who gave it, when, and a photograph if there is something to see.

Ten seconds on the day turns a verbal instruction into a priced variation instead of a disagreement in March.

Corporate Brochure09
The handover standard

Evidence, not promises.

The riskiest moment in any drywall package is the day a wall is boarded over. Once it is closed, nobody can see what is behind it — and years later, somebody has to prove what was built. We decided that proof should be part of the product.

1

Every wall has a number

Assigned at tender stage and kept through construction into the handover file. The wall we priced, built and are claiming for is provably the same wall.

2

Every stage is inspected and signed

Setting out, framing, services closed in, insulation, each layer of board, fire-stopping, decoration — dated and initialled by the supervisor who looked at it.

3

Photographed before it disappears

No wall is boarded without a photograph of the services inside it. No fire-stopping is signed off without a picture of the seal.

4

Handed over as a pack, not a promise

Every wall, every check, every photograph, as one document your team can file, audit and rely on — per zone where the works are zoned.

What it costs you

Nothing. It is how we work. The systems are ours, built in-house, and they travel with every EDM package as standard. What we ask in return is that the inspection and test plan and the benchmark standard are agreed early, so the record measures the right things against a standard both parties have signed.

Corporate Brochure10
Safety

We describe our controls rather than score them.

A lot of construction safety communication is a number and a slogan. Neither tells a main contractor's HSE team anything they can check, so we set out the system instead.

Before work starts
Method statements, risk assessments and material approvals submitted and signed off in advance. Nothing starts on a verbal.
Every day
Operatives inducted, directly supervised and briefed at daily toolbox talks — specific to the floor and the day. What has changed since yesterday, who is working above, where the new penetration is, what the permit covers and what it doesn't.
In live environments
Permit discipline, restricted access and out-of-hours working handled as normal conditions rather than exceptions.
On the record
Full HSE documentation, insurances and supporting records set out in our PQQ information pack, available on request.
A toolbox talk that opens with "safety is everyone's responsibility" has already lost the room. The ones that work are specific, short, and treated as real by somebody senior.

Housekeeping belongs in this section too. Material stacked rather than dumped, offcuts cleared, edge protection in place. It is the most reliable indicator of how a site is being run, and it is visible within ten minutes of walking a floor.

Corporate Brochure11
Our people

The workforce is not a cost line. It is the product.

In a self-delivery business, everything a client is buying arrives on site in a van. Which is why our labour is directly employed rather than brokered through a chain of suppliers with our logo on the hoarding.

Known outputs. We have measured the same crews over years, so the programme we sign up to is one we have genuinely resourced rather than one we hope to achieve.

A standard that holds. Quality is not the standard of the best wall on the job. It is the standard of the four hundredth, built on a Thursday afternoon by a crew who are behind. That is set by supervision, not by intent.

Answers from someone who was there. A question about a detail on Level 4 goes to the supervisor who signed it off, not down a chain to a supplier who demobilised a fortnight ago.

Teams that self-correct. Crews who have worked together for years notice the setting out is off before the third wall goes up, and flag a detail that won't work before it is built four hundred times. That is worth more than any individual's skill.

Supervision is European led and European managed, and the workforce scales with the package rather than being assembled for it.
