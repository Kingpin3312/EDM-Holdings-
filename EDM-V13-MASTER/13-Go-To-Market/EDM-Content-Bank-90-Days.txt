EDM HOLDINGS — CONTENT BANK, FIRST 90 DAYS
One post a week for twelve weeks, starting after the three launch posts.
Post from the company page. Vary the day and time. If a week gets busy on
site, skip it rather than post something thin — a gap looks human, filler
doesn't. Photos from live jobs make every one of these stronger; add them
as they come.

------------------------------------------------------------
WEEK 1 — Why every wall gets a number
------------------------------------------------------------
Every wall on our jobs has a reference. W07 on the takeoff is W07 on the
drawing in the cabin, W07 in the inspection record, and W07 in the
handover file.

Sounds like admin. It isn't. When a question comes up two years after
handover, the answer is one search away instead of one opening-up
exercise away.

Small habit. Big difference at the end of a job.

------------------------------------------------------------
WEEK 2 — The photo before the board goes on
------------------------------------------------------------
The most expensive moment in drywall is the one nobody sees: the minute
before a wall gets boarded over.

Everything inside it disappears. The services, the insulation, the fire
detail. From that point on, proving what's behind the board means cutting
a hole in it.

So we take the photograph first. Every wall, before it closes. Our
supervisors can't sign the stage off without it.

Cheap insurance. Ask anyone who's had to open up a corridor.

------------------------------------------------------------
WEEK 3 — On splitting the package
------------------------------------------------------------
A question for the main contractors: how many subcontractors are inside
your typical interior package? Partitions with one firm, ceilings with
another, joinery a third, painting a fourth.

Every split creates an interface. And every interface is somewhere for
programme to slip and quality arguments to start. When it goes wrong,
three firms point at each other and you inherit the referee's job.

We price the whole interior and deliver it with our own people. One
contract. One team on site. One name to ring.

------------------------------------------------------------
WEEK 4 — Since 1986
------------------------------------------------------------
This business started in 1986. Different name back then, same trade,
and a fair few of the same faces.

Nearly four decades of drywall teaches you things a brochure can't:
which details fail, which programmes were never real, and why the
cheapest tender usually costs the most by handover.

We've carried all of it into EDM Holdings. The name is newer than the
scars.

------------------------------------------------------------
WEEK 5 — The Friday number
------------------------------------------------------------
"How complete is Level 3?"

Most subcontractors answer that from a walk-round and a feeling. Our
answer comes from the inspection records — a stage only counts when a
supervisor has signed it, so the percentage in our weekly report is the
percentage that's actually built.

Main contractors notice the difference around week six, when everyone
else's numbers start drifting and ours don't.

------------------------------------------------------------
WEEK 6 — The check nobody sees
------------------------------------------------------------
Fire-stopping is the least visible work on a fit-out and the most
important. Head-of-wall details, sealed penetrations, the right product
in the right gap. Once decoration's done, all of it is hidden.

On our jobs it's photographed and signed before it disappears, wall by
wall, and the whole record goes to the client at handover.

Regulations now expect a golden thread of building information. Interior
walls are where that thread usually breaks. Not on ours.

------------------------------------------------------------
WEEK 7 — Twenty years on the tools
------------------------------------------------------------
Our Project Director, Kenny Buchanan, has run drywall and fit-out
packages for twenty years — Ireland, London, the UAE, Australia.

Ask him what all that time actually taught him and you won't get a
philosophy. You'll get specifics. Which week a programme really starts
slipping. What a fixer needs to hold his rate without cutting corners.
Why the second visit to a snag costs three times the first.

That knowledge doesn't come from a course. It walks onto site every
morning, and it's the part of our price you can't buy anywhere else.

------------------------------------------------------------
WEEK 8 — What the collapses taught us
------------------------------------------------------------
The UK has watched two giants of this industry fail in a decade, and
both took their supply chains down with them. Thin margins, everything
subcontracted, risk passed down until someone at the bottom couldn't
carry it.

We took a different lesson than most. Directly employed labour costs
more on paper. It also means we know our real costs, our people turn up,
and our promises are ours to keep — not a sub-subcontractor's.

Resilience isn't exciting. Neither is still being here in forty years.
We'll take it.

------------------------------------------------------------
WEEK 9 — What we look for in your drawings
------------------------------------------------------------
When a drawing package lands with our QS, the price is the last thing
worked out. First come the questions: does the head detail survive the
slab deflection, are the fire strategy and the partition schedule telling
the same story, and is there physically room in the riser walls for what
the services drawings want to put through them.

Catching those at tender costs nothing. Catching them on site costs
weeks. A good drywall price is mostly good reading.

------------------------------------------------------------
WEEK 10 — One standard, wherever the wall is
------------------------------------------------------------
Our supervisors learned this trade on sites in the UK and Ireland. They
now run packages here in the UAE. Different regulations, different
climate, one thing they carried over unchanged: the inspection standard.

The same eight checks, the same photo rules before a wall closes, the
same handover pack. Regulations you adopt. Standards you carry.

------------------------------------------------------------
WEEK 11 — Always looking
------------------------------------------------------------
An honest note on hiring. We're a self-delivery contractor, which means
our tradespeople are the product — so we're always interested in good
fixers, tapers, ceiling hands and supervisors in the markets we work.

What we offer is steady work with one employer, proper supervision, and
sites where the paperwork protects the people doing the job rather than
chasing them.

If that sounds like your kind of site: enquiries@edmholdings.ae.

------------------------------------------------------------
WEEK 12 — What "done" means
------------------------------------------------------------
Every trade has its own definition of finished. Here's ours.

Done means the last wall is signed through every stage. Done means the
fire records are photographed and filed. Done means the handover pack is
on the client's desk and the snag list is empty because the snags were
caught while the trades were still on the floor.

Done, in other words, means nobody has to ring us afterwards. Though
plenty do — usually about the next job.
