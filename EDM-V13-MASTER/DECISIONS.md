# DECISION 1 — The turnover figure comes out of every public document.

**What is on record, twice, differently.**

The capability statement prints: *£21m+ average annual turnover (AED 90m+), 11
schemes a year, 4 sectors*, described as "the business's annual numbers, built
on our E&D Drywall heritage".

Also on record: the E&D Drywall track record was *£20.31m+ across 11 schemes in
the UK and Ireland* — a total, across a number of schemes, not a yearly rate.

Those are not two phrasings of one fact. If £20.31m across 11 schemes is the
underlying data, then "£21m+ average annual turnover, 11 schemes a year"
restates a lifetime total as an annual run-rate. That is roughly an order of
magnitude, and it is printed on the document that goes to procurement.

**The decision: remove the money figure from all public-facing material.**

Not softened. Removed. Three reasons.

Firstly, it cannot survive verification. A Tier-1 PQQ asks for two or three
years of audited accounts. If those accounts do not show £21m+ a year, the claim
does not merely fail — it reads as inflation, and everything else in the
document gets read again more carefully.

Secondly, it is not earning anything. Nobody shortlists a specialist
subcontractor on turnover. They shortlist on evidence of delivery, licensed
scope, insurance limits and references. The figure is doing no work and carrying
all the risk.

Thirdly, it is unnecessary. The genuinely persuasive numbers in this business
are thirteen zones on a mission-critical facility and a published handover
standard. Those are checkable and rare.

**Replacement copy for the capability statement track-record block:**

Replace the four-figure strip and its caption with:

> **A record that survives diligence.**
>
> Four sectors: residential, workplace, hotels and leisure, and education.
> Eleven delivered schemes across the UK and Ireland behind this team, and live
> delivery in the UAE including a thirteen-zone data centre package in Ajman.
>
> Turnover, audited accounts, insurance limits and package-specific references
> are in our PQQ information pack, available on request.

That last line is stronger than a number, because it says the evidence exists
and invites the check.

**Where the figure may still be used:** the PQQ pack, alongside the accounts
that support it, stated in whatever form the accounts actually support.

# DECISION 2 — "100% safety record" comes out.

Currently printed in the capability statement and the corporate brochure as a
headline statistic: *100% — safety record, held across every EDM site.*

**The decision: remove it.**

An unqualified absolute over an unbounded period cannot be evidenced and can
only ever be disproved. A Tier-1 HSE team reads "100%" and asks three questions:
over what period, measured how, and can we see the RIDDOR or equivalent returns.
There is no answer to that which is as strong as the claim, so the claim is a
net loss.

It is also unnecessary, because it sits directly beneath a paragraph that is
already excellent and already true:

> Method statements, risk assessments and material approvals are signed off
> before work starts. Operatives are inducted, directly supervised and briefed
> at daily toolbox talks. Stage inspections run against agreed inspection and
> test plans.

That paragraph is more persuasive to a safety professional than any percentage,
because it describes a system rather than an outcome.

**Replacement for the statistic pair:**

> **Directly employed** — inducted, supervised and briefed daily, on every site.
>
> **EU-led** — European-managed supervision, fully scalable workforce.
>
> Full HSE documentation, insurances and records in our PQQ pack, on request.

**If the underlying fact is real and you want to keep it**, the only defensible
form is bounded and dated: *"No reportable incidents across EDM sites to [date]"*
— with the definition of reportable stated and the returns available. Do not use
"100%".

# DECISION 3 — Delivery is the UAE. The rest is the team's track record.

On record: the London, Ireland and Australia companies are separate from the UAE
business and were established as part of a credibility exercise. They are not
UAE-entity delivery capability, and EDM Technical Services L.L.C must not be
presented as able to mobilise labour in those markets.

**The decision: one form of words everywhere.**

- **Delivery:** the UAE, through EDM Technical Services L.L.C.
- **Track record:** the team's, built across the UK, Ireland and the UAE.
- **Structured fields** (LinkedIn locations, schema.org, directory listings):
  Dubai only.

Applied in V12 to every page of the website and to all LinkedIn copy. The
service pages still describe the trades; what has gone is the claim that the
UAE entity delivers them in four countries.

**Why this is also better commercially:** "forty years of this trade, now
delivering here" is a stronger and simpler story than a list of four countries
a procurement manager can start asking questions about.
